2026-11-11 Evidence for intracellular memory storage and its implications for AI David L. Glanzman (guest talk to the Levin lab), with discussion with Michael Levin A neuroscientist argues that memory is stored inside neurons rather than at synapses, and describes an AI system (Naresa, in a paper he says is on arXiv, so a preprint) in which each unit keeps its own transferable memory. Q&A covers the Church-Turing thesis and what randomness adds.

Week of 2026-11-05: new in the feed

Captions came through for all three new videos, so each has a summary page.

Published Video Who In brief
2026-11-05 Is consciousness popularity? Narcis Marincat (guest talk to the Levin lab), with discussion with Michael Levin Narcis Marincat, who studied psychology and neuroscience and later computer science and works in AI, presents his "popularity theory of consciousness". He starts from two ideas: cells, including neurons, are cognitive agents, and human society can be seen as an organism made of people. He then defines consciousness as the process by which information made by an organism's basic units becomes popular within that organism, and applies it to binding, the unconscious, the self and the hard problem. In the discussion Levin talks about frame rates of minds, training sets of logic sentences, a "boredom theory of aging" his lab is testing, and why he doubts that most of the universe is unconscious.
2026-10-31 Thoughts as Thinkers: biology & computer science of inspiration within & without us Michael Levin (talk given in a joint session; he says he is presenting "together with Chris") A 35-minute talk on what Levin calls "free lunches": abilities a system shows that were not paid for by design, natural selection or training. He walks through examples at every scale, from people with very little brain tissue and tadpoles that see through an eye on their tail, to molecular networks that learn, xenobots and anthrobots, and simple sorting algorithms that do things nobody coded. He argues these abilities come from an ordered space of patterns (the Platonic space) that should be studied systematically, and that inspiration in human creatives is the same thing at a higher level. This is a different upload from the 2026-10-16 "Thoughts as Thinkers" talk on this site.
2026-10-30 Conversation #1 with Martin Šolc: the law and diverse intelligence Martin Šolc (postdoc, Charles University Faculty of Law) and Michael Levin A legal scholar who works on civil and medical law asks how the law should sort new kinds of entities, such as AIs, cyborgs and animals, and says Levin's TAME framework (a way of measuring cognitive capacity whatever a being is made of) could help. He proposes four "legal recognition levels", from a plain thing up to full legal personhood. Levin argues our who-or-what language is too crude for a coming world of cyborgs and augmented people, that capability and the ability to suffer should be kept apart, and that the trait worth measuring may be the size of a being's "cone of compassion".

Week of 2026-10-29: new in the feed

Captions came through this week, so every new video has a summary page, and so do four earlier videos that were missing one (marked "earlier").

Published Video Who In brief
2026-10-29 Biomedical Synthetic Biology Lior Nissim (guest talk to the Levin lab), with discussion Lior Nissim, who runs a synthetic biology lab at the Hadassah medical center in Israel ("Dasa" in the captions), explains how his group engineers cells the way engineers build autonomous electronic systems: sensors, a processor and outputs. Most of the talk is about synthetic promoters, short DNA switches built to switch on only in certain cell states, and gene circuits that do logic inside cells.
2026-10-28 Physical Intelligence: thermodynamic roots of agency Benjamin De Bari-Cheung (Thomas Jefferson University), guest talk with discussion An ecological psychologist presents the "physical intelligence" program from his graduate work at the University of Connecticut, which looks for the roots of agency in thermodynamics. His main example is a non-living electrical system that grows tree-like structures, which he analyzes with the idea that systems tend toward the fastest rate of entropy production their circumstances allow.
2026-10-26 Perturbing Complex Networks Debanik Bhattacharjee (postdoc, Hebrew University), guest talk with discussion A postdoc whose PhD was on fluid flow through porous materials shows how he studies complex systems by disturbing them and reading the response. Most of the talk uses pore networks (oil and water moving through a chip of pillars) as a test bed, and he says he is starting to carry the same approach over to neural systems, with early results not ready to show.
2026-10-25 From Top-Down Physics to Diverse Intelligence Chris Fields (talk to the Levin lab group) Chris Fields builds on a forthcoming paper in the journal Mind and Matter ("Embodied Cognition, the Free Energy Principle, and the Unity of Science") that argues against physicalism, and places it in the lab's current Platonic-space discussions. He goes from the Platonic space to the space of current mathematics, to physics seen from the top down, to each observer's experiential world, and ends at panpsychism.
2026-10-17 (earlier) New Bodies, Unconventional Minds: a continuum of cognition (& consciousness) Michael Levin A short conference talk where Levin makes three claims: the focus on neurons is too narrow, the same reasons we credit other people with consciousness apply to many structures in the body, and physicalism is false "all the way down". He walks through learning in small molecular networks, xenobots and anthrobots, and his Platonic-space proposal, then closes with personal views on consciousness that he says he can't prove.
2026-10-17 (earlier) Decoding and Translating the Languages of Biology: towards de novo cell design Jian Shu (guest talk to the Levin lab) Jian Shu presents tools for measuring and redesigning cell states, aiming at designing new cells from scratch. He starts from iPS-cell reprogramming, moves to methods that use optimal transport on single-cell data to trace how cells change over time, and then to methods that predict gene expression from label-free images (Raman to RNA). He closes by calling for a shared, organized dataset of cell images and measurements so AI can suggest the next experiment.
2026-10-16 (earlier) Thoughts as Thinkers: the biology & computer science of inspiration Michael Levin Levin describes a research program to study inspiration and intuition in the lab. His two points: there is a non-physical component that standard physical and computational models don't capture, and inspirations and the agents who get inspired are not different kinds of thing but points on one continuum.
2026-09-02 (earlier) Deciphering Visceral Instincts Diego Bohórquez (guest talk) Diego Bohórquez, who grew up in the Amazon region of Ecuador, explains how the gut senses what we eat and tells the brain in real time. His lab found that certain gut lining cells have long arms that connect to nerves; he named them neuropods. Silencing them leaves mice unable to tell sugar apart, and a microbial protein sensed by these cells reduces appetite, which the lab calls the neurobiotic sense.
