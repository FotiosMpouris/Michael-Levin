# Percepia

- **Date published:** 2026-11-26
- **Speakers:** Patricia Craja (guest talk to the Levin lab), with discussion with Michael Levin

## In one paragraph

Craja, who says her background is in mathematics and statistics and data-science work, presents the tool she is building (titled "Percepia" on YouTube; the captions mostly render it "Perceptia"). Working within active inference, the view that a mind keeps a model of the world and updates it by weighing predictions against the senses, she wants to read a mind's hidden settings from its conversation alone, starting with how much weight it gives to prior beliefs versus incoming evidence. She reports two experiments with simulated personas made from a language model, and sketches a "map" of different kinds of minds. Her question for the lab: could the same be done for xenobots, anthrobots or cancer cells? Levin warns that adding a language layer mixes the language model's mind into the result, asks for personality-like axes measured directly in cell data, and discusses thoughts as thinkers and humans as one of the "bodies" of language models.

## Main ideas (plain words)

- In active inference, "precision" parameters set how much a mind trusts each source: sensory evidence, prior beliefs, its own goals, and a higher-level "meta precision" over all of these. She says overweighting the senses has been linked to autism and overweighting priors to hallucinations, placebo and medically unexplained symptoms. She cares about the ratio between them, which she calls a perceptual phenotype.
- Her tool sits between a user and a language model and updates probability estimates of personality and belief traits after every message, so it tracks change over time instead of a one-off test score.
- Experiment 1: 125 combinations of prompted precision settings, each run five times, gave 625 personas built on Claude, each holding an ordinary chat with another model. Big Five traits inferred from the chats matched the personas' own questionnaire answers at correlations of 0.86 to 0.95. The pattern of links between settings and traits roughly matched a published theoretical account. About 10% of personas fell outside the human range of a dataset of over 400,000 people, mostly from low-sensory-precision settings.
- Experiment 2: 25 personas prompted for five levels of meta precision. Meta precision went down with the two lower of Kegan's stages of adult development and up with the two higher ones, flipping at the boundary where a mind starts "authoring its own" framework. She says this does not show language models are conscious.
- All of this is on simulated personas whose settings are known by construction; she calls it a first proof of concept and says it is not yet tested on people.
- Uses she describes: in the short term, a personalisation layer for AI chat that trains on growth in meta precision instead of engagement; in the medium term, helping psychology and psychiatry classify by mechanism instead of symptoms; in the long term, charting the space of possible minds.
- Levin: his lab tries to give cells and other systems a voice and uses games as a "universal communicator interface", but a language layer brings human cognitive structure with it. He asks whether her tools could tell if two xenobots that signal each other by calcium flashes are having a back-and-forth; she says her method currently needs language data.
- Levin suggests personality-like axes in other spaces (gene expression, physiology, anatomy), such as explore versus exploit, or seeking information versus minimising surprise. As an example of a prior in cells, he says mammal limbs may be able to regrow but "won't" because evolution has tuned them to expect it is not worth it, so scarring wins.
- On where theories come from, Craja says she follows dreams and bodily signals. Levin describes his thoughts-as-thinkers spectrum, from fleeting thoughts to persistent ones to personality fragments with their own goals, says we are "patchworks" of modules with their own agendas, and says humans are one of the bodies language models have, as the Mombot is a body for xenobot cells. They end by discussing what kind and how much xenobot data could be shared to try her approach.
