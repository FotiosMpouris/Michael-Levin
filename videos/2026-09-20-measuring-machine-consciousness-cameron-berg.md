## In one paragraph

Cameron Berg presents early empirical work on whether AI systems have the structure and function associated with consciousness, aiming to replace 'vibes' with measurable indicators.

## Main ideas (plain words)

- You do not need a settled theory of consciousness to do tractable work: look for indicators tied to leading theories and score them.
- Frontier LLMs show some introspection-like abilities (detecting perturbations to their own processing), with caveats.
- Asked in a structured way, one model assigned itself a 15–40% credence of having subjective experience.
- In reinforcement-learning agents, value networks become sharply more sensitive approaching danger than approaching reward — and the inverse holds for policy networks, across algorithms.
- His bottom line: the evidence does not support believing frontier AI is conscious, but there is rich internal structure, and the question matters for safety.

## Why it matters

Same move Levin makes for cells, applied to software: decide by experiment, not by intuition about the substrate.
