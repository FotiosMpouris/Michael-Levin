# Biomedical Synthetic Biology

## In one paragraph

Lior Nissim, who runs a synthetic biology lab at the Hadassah medical center in Israel ("Dasa" in the captions), explains how his group engineers cells the way engineers build autonomous electronic systems: sensors, a processor and outputs. Most of the talk is about synthetic promoters, short DNA switches built to switch on only in certain cell states, and gene circuits that do logic inside cells.

## Main ideas (plain words)

- He separates bioengineering (copying existing genes and parts) from synthetic biology (building new genetic parts and circuits that did not exist before).
- Inputs are mostly synthetic promoters: short stretches of DNA (around 100 to 250 base pairs) made of repeats of one transcription-factor binding site, so they turn on in very specific cell states.
- Processors are gene circuits that compute logic in cells, for example an XOR-style circuit built from two promoters that suppress each other's output.
- His lab applies these platforms to cancer, immune cell therapy, chemotherapy resistance, vaccines and ALS. He says the screening data analysis is hard and machine learning is helping.
- In the discussion he says electrical engineers, computer scientists and synthetic biologists should work together more.
