# Neuroscience Beyond Neurons: Communicating with Unconventional Minds

## In one paragraph

Levin argues neuroscience is really the science of how minds work in any substrate, and walks through the evidence that cells and tissues show the hallmarks of intelligence — and that bioelectricity is how we can talk to them.

## Main ideas (plain words)

- 'Mind blindness': like all observers, we only recognize intelligence at our own scale of space and time. Whether something is intelligent is an experimental question, not an armchair one.
- He uses William James's definition of intelligence — reaching the same goal by different means — because it is testable: put a barrier in the way and see what the system does.
- Development shows the hallmarks: homeostasis, reaching the same anatomy by new paths, and top-down control (an abstract goal ends up moving ions across membranes).
- Bioelectric pattern memories are instructive and rewritable. Planaria edited to grow two heads keep regenerating two heads with unchanged DNA; the 'electric face' pre-pattern shows where the face will form before it does. Neuroscience tools (optogenetics, ion-channel drugs) work outside the brain.
- Anthrobots — self-assembled from adult human airway cells — can heal a scratch across a layer of human neurons. Levin closes on the coming world of cyborgs, hybrids and chimeras, and the need for an ethical 'synthbiosis'.

## Why it matters

This is the clearest single statement of the core thesis: bioelectricity is the interface to the body's own intelligence, which is what makes regeneration and cancer reprogramming plausible as medicine.
