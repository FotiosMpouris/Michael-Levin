# Thermodynamics, Control, & Fundamental Niche

- **Date published:** 2026-10-10
- **Speakers:** M. Kearney, I. Shames, R. Schouten, P. Egré, R. Evans (guests); Levin and lab in discussion

## In one paragraph

An ecologist, two control engineers and a philosopher present early work linking an organism's heat and water budget (thermodynamics) with control theory (how it regulates itself) to define the 'fundamental niche' — the range of conditions where it can survive.

## Main ideas (plain words)

- Living things are heat engines held far from equilibrium; the higher the metabolic rate, the further from equilibrium.
- A habitat is not an environment: a dragon lizard, a gecko and a grasshopper share one habitat but each builds a different environment through its behavior and physiology.
- Control theory treats a body as boxes with inputs, outputs and disturbances, and it brings hard limits — e.g., you must respond fast enough to control a fast system, whatever the system is.
- Levin's lab connects this to information: coordination and communication eat a large share of a developing organism's energy budget.

## Why it matters

Levin's view of cells as goal-seeking agents needs a physics of cost: what does it take, in energy, to hold a goal? This talk supplies the engineering vocabulary (feasible sets, fundamental limits) for that question.
