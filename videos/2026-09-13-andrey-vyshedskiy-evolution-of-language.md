## In one paragraph

Neuroscientist Andrey Vyshedskiy explains his theory that complex, syntactic imagination depends on brain networks built by conversation in early childhood, and what that implies for human evolution and for children who miss the window.

## Main ideas (plain words)

- The long front-to-back brain networks for complex language develop after birth, through conversation and storytelling during a sensitive period.
- Stories with a subject and an object ('who did what to whom') exercise the imagination those networks need.
- Children not exposed in time may never acquire full syntactic language; reopening that window would be a major therapy target.
- Levin asks what makes the period sensitive and notes bioelectric depolarization can push mature neurons back into the cell cycle — an example of how his tools might touch such questions, without claiming they are the answer.

## Why it matters

Ties brain development to a concrete intervention window, and shows Levin probing where bioelectric tools might reopen plasticity.
