# Decoding and Translating the Languages of Biology: towards de novo cell design

- **Date published:** 2026-10-17
- **Speakers:** Jian Shu (guest talk to the Levin lab)
- **Format:** Guest talk

## In one paragraph

Jian Shu presents tools for measuring and redesigning cell states, aiming at designing new cells from scratch. He starts from iPS-cell reprogramming, moves to methods that use optimal transport on single-cell data to trace how cells change over time, and then to methods that predict gene expression from label-free images (Raman to RNA). He closes by calling for a shared, organized dataset of cell images and measurements so AI can suggest the next experiment.

## Main ideas (plain words)

- His PhD work found stem cells can be made by balancing rival lineage factors (a "seesaw" model), not only with the classic stem-cell factors.
- Waddington-OT uses optimal transport to link single-cell snapshots across days and reconstruct cell trajectories.
- Raman to RNA predicts single-cell gene expression from Raman imaging, a label-free way to look at living cells. Lipids turned out enriched in senescent cells.
- He proposes a community resource like ImageNet or the Protein Data Bank, but for cells.
