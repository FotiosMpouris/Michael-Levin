## In one paragraph

A gut-brain neuroscientist and a neurologist-engineer talk with Levin about how information crosses levels — from gut sensor cells to the brain, and from neurons in a dish to clinical tools.

## Main ideas (plain words)

- Bohórquez's lab found gut sensor cells wired to the vagus nerve that rapidly sense nutrients and signal the brain to guide decisions.
- Serruya grows rat and human neurons on 2D and 3D electrode arrays and asks how a clinician can 'talk' to cells in a language they respond to.
- Levin's frame: much information may be unavailable only because we lack the right interface; hypnosis and similar methods might soften the boundaries between levels.
- His lab is already trying to put a language layer on top of biology — talking to gene regulatory networks in English — with some success.
- Next step he proposes: each writes down minimal models that would answer one shared question.

## Why it matters

This is the interface question in its most practical form, and the language-layer idea is where LLM agents and Levin's biology meet.
