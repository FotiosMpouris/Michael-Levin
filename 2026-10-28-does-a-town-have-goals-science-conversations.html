In one paragraph

A conversation about collectives at every scale. Levin argues that "rules" describe a system well only at the low end of what he calls the spectrum of persuadability; for smarter systems you need goals, priors and the system's own point of view. He says his lab changes what cells do by communicating with them rather than rewriting their rules, sketches unpublished work on "pointer arithmetic" in Platonic space, entertains the idea that towns and companies may have goals of their own (as an empirical question), and offers a conjecture on why patterns from Platonic space ingress into the physical world. He closes with personal advice for people with unconventional ideas, including why he waited almost 40 years to talk publicly about Platonic space.

Main ideas (plain words)

  • Intelligence at each level is an experimental claim. Levin stresses that saying a given level has some intelligence is "an empirical claim", not a definition: you have to test each system.
  • Rules work only at the low end. Fixed rules (truth tables, cellular automata) suit simple systems. For systems that change their own structure, the number of players or the problem space, rules become "impoverished" and the right tools look more like psychoanalysis, love and friendship. His example: a bowling ball on a bumpy landscape can be described from outside, a mouse cannot; you need the mouse's view. He cites Randall Beer's paper on the cognitive domain of a Game of Life glider and Richard Watson's phrase "a mutually vulnerable relationship".
  • Communicating, not rewriting. In his lab's work, such as the two-headed planarians, "We don't try to change the rules." They rewrite goal states, give stimuli and train. He says most of the field (genetic engineering, synthetic biology, protein design) changes the rules, and that this leaves too much on the table.
  • Pointer arithmetic in Platonic space. If physical bodies are "pointers" into a space of policies, he wants a way to combine small pointers into bigger ones and predict ahead of time what pattern a collective will pull in. He says none of this is published yet.
  • The letter from the town. A letter from the town does not come from the clerk, and possibly no single person in the town agrees with it. Whether social, financial and corporate structures have their own goals is plausible to him but needs perturbative experiments, which are hard and often unethical at that scale. He describes a pre-COVID experiment design to see whether an ant colony, not the individual ants, could learn a food reward, and a "gravitational synapse" the size of a solar system that he and a physicist friend tried to design.
  • Can Platonic space change? Flagged by him as conjecture with no strong claims: the patterns are not a fixed tree of possibilities but subsystems whose properties can change. His suspicion is that they are under strong pressure to ingress because the physical world gives them a chance to grow and change. Some, like the digits of e, may be "the rocks of that world"; others, like kinds of minds, may change over time.
  • Advice for unconventional ideas. Ask what you are asking others to give up for your idea; it is your job to make it irresistible; grow a thick skin; nobody is against you. He describes his own path in steps, from ion channel genes in development to voltage gradients, then computation, then cognition, each grant building on the last. Evidence is necessary but not sufficient: people also need a story that lets them take the evidence in.

Why it matters

The interview spells out, as a choice of method, Levin's stance of communicating with cells rather than rewriting their rules, and treats collectives above the organism (towns, companies, the scientific community) as open experimental questions rather than metaphors. That extends the scaling story on Basal Cognition and Cognitive Lightcone upward, and adds a new, explicitly speculative claim about change inside Platonic Space (Biological Interpretation).
