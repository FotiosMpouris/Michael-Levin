Agency & Intelligence Across Scales (Levin, Friston & Chis-Ciure)

  • Date published: 2026-11-18
  • Channel: Wandering Thoughts (a podcast channel, not the Levin lab channel)
  • Speakers: Michael Levin, Karl Friston and Robert Chis-Ciure, with the show's host
  • Format: Guest roundtable (1 h 25 min). Levin takes part in roughly the first 58 minutes, then leaves for another commitment; Friston and Chis-Ciure continue.
  • Watch: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EY0nrb7AXJs
  • Topics: Platonic space and ingressing minds, Xenobots and anthrobots, Cognition in cells, AI and biology

In one paragraph

The host asks how Friston's free energy principle and active inference connect to Levin's work on goal-directed cells. Levin frames the central question as where the properties of new kinds of beings come from when they were not designed, selected or trained, using xenobots and anthrobots as test cases. Friston answers in the language of physics: boundary conditions, scale invariance and the idea that a slow, higher scale sets the space of futures a faster, lower scale can explore. Chis-Ciure describes his 2025 work with Levin on measuring intelligence as search efficiency. The three then work through "free lunches", whether patterns can be causal, and whether almost everything might be a free lunch. After Levin leaves, Friston and Chis-Ciure define intelligence, agency, sentience and consciousness and discuss emergence.

Main ideas (plain words)

  • Where novel properties come from. Levin says we usually explain a system's abilities by engineering, evolution or training, and he builds model systems to stress-test that. Xenobots and anthrobots are made from ordinary cells, show new gene expression and new behaviors, and were never selected for. He rejects the answer "it was emergent" and wants a predictive science of the space those properties come from.
  • The "xenobot home world". Levin describes a pipeline he built that works backwards from xenobot gene expression to guess what environment would have selected for it. The inferred world is "not terribly different" from ours but different. He wonders whether it describes their future rather than a past, and says plainly this is early work and he is making no real claims.
  • Friston's physics reading. Anything that persists has to obey certain boundary conditions, which he links to the Helmholtz-Hodge decomposition: a gradient flow that resists dissipation plus a circular, non-dissipative flow that shows up as rhythms at every scale. Because the higher scale changes so slowly, from below it looks timeless, so it can specify in advance the futures a xenobot is equipped for. He calls Levin's idea "a beautiful idea".
  • Measuring intelligence as search efficiency. Chis-Ciure describes a 2025 paper with Levin that compares biological search with a random walk. He reports two to three orders of magnitude better efficiency for amoebas and about 21 orders of magnitude for planarian head regeneration under barium, even with conservative estimates, and says the random-walk baseline now looks problematic to him.
  • Free lunches as a ledger. Levin's minimal definition: total up everything paid for by design, selection and learning, and whatever competence is left over needs explaining. He credits older ideas like Stuart Kauffman's "order for free", and adds that some leftover patterns encode problem-solving policies, which you find by giving them a body and testing behavior.
  • Patterns as causal controllers. Friston links Levin's "ingression" to the good regulator theorem: to act on a world, a system has to embody a model of it. Levin says the causal role of mathematical patterns is a live debate and that he may try to build some of this into a model with them.
  • Is everything a free lunch? Friston asks whether, taken seriously, almost everything counts. Levin says Chris Fields made a similar point by email, that he is only claiming a handful of demonstrated cases, but that once a fourth option is on the table you start re-looking at everything.
  • After Levin leaves. Friston draws a line between autonomous systems and agents that plan, and says large language models currently act without intentions. Chis-Ciure separates intelligence from felt experience and calls unquantified "emergence" an empty placeholder. Friston explains how scales relate using the renormalization group, with a traffic-jam example.

Why it matters

It puts Levin's Platonic-space program side by side with Friston's free energy principle and shows where they line up and where they do not. Friston offers a physics route to the same "where do the properties come from" question without any extra space, while Levin keeps the question open as an engineering problem. The Chis-Ciure efficiency numbers are one of the few attempts to put a number on basal cognition. See also Platonic Space (Biological Interpretation) and Cognitive Lightcone.
