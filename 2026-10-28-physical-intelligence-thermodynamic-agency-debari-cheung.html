Physical Intelligence: thermodynamic roots of agency

  • Date published: 2026-10-28
  • Speakers: Benjamin De Bari-Cheung (Thomas Jefferson University), guest talk with discussion
  • Format: Guest talk
  • Watch: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eCIF_h7qwME
  • Topics: Control and energy, Cognition in cells

In one paragraph

An ecological psychologist presents the "physical intelligence" program from his graduate work at the University of Connecticut, which looks for the roots of agency in thermodynamics. His main example is a non-living electrical system that grows tree-like structures, which he analyzes with the idea that systems tend toward the fastest rate of entropy production their circumstances allow.

Main ideas (plain words)

  • Ecological psychology aims for "a psychology for all organisms": explain behavior in terms that apply to birds and amoebas, not just humans (he cites Hume on this).
  • Far from equilibrium, systems may follow principles such as maximum rate of entropy production. That is the sense of goal-directedness he works with.
  • In the electrical tree system, structure forms as entropy production rises, oscillations appear on their own, and when one tree is locked down the other changes its dynamics in a way that compensates for the lost current.
  • In discussion he says he does not think experience is universal; he sees it as emergent, and thinks systems like these are probably close to where that line would be.
